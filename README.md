All datasets were **ethically sourced**, and systematically **cleaned and restructured** to ensure consistency, eliminate redundancy, and prevent potential bias against any demographic group. Neighborhoods were assigned their own unique identifier, any repeatable attributes were separated into reference tables, and various fields were consolidated or removed to support analytical queries. The resulting database provides a **reliable framework for exploring relationships between neighborhood characteristics and crime activity in Boston**. \\

Using this database, we were able to efficiently run queries that revealed key patterns in arrests, homicides, and incident reports across neighborhoods. Our key findings included:\
- Motor vehicle crashes, investigate-person calls, and medical assistance requests were the
most common incident types\
- Drug-related arrests and homicides were concentrated primarily in Roxbury, Dorchester,
and Downtown\
- A weak correlation exists between demographic indicators such as median income or
foreign born population, and crime metrics\
